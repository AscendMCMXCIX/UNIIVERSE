# Team Leaderboards

Friendly competition on the team's own Riot games: **Team → Leaderboards** (`/leaderboards`), between Players and Matches. The rules, and why, are in `docs/LEADERBOARDS_MODEL.md`.

## The page

One page since 19 Nov 2026, with no views or tabs to switch between: Recent form leads (how members are doing lately), the season's records and achievements follow (what they have done, which rewards playing more). Everything is built from the one load of the team's data (see *Updates and caching*); neither part has a query of its own.

## The multikill score

What the multikill board ranks (Release 15, 23 Nov 2026, at the owner's request: "a fairer overall multikill ranking, not most pentakills wins"). The code is `src/domain/multikill-score.ts` (pure functions, worked examples in its tests), the board in `src/domain/leaderboard-records.ts` (`buildMultikillBoard`), the comparison's query in `src/server/leaderboards/multikill-baseline.ts`.

**In plain words.** How often a member makes multikills, per game, against what players on the same roles and champions make in the same stored games. 100 is the usual; 130 is 30% more. Every multikill counts once, at its largest, worth its kills (a double kill 2, a pentakill 5), so a rarer kind counts for more. With few games a score stays close to 100, so one lucky game cannot decide the board, and the more games, the less a score owes to chance. The board and the profiles show each multikill once, as the score counts it; the score never credits a multikill nobody made.

**1. Riot's counters, each multikill once.** Riot keeps four counters per player and game (`doubleKills`, `tripleKills`, `quadraKills`, `pentaKills`, with `largestMultiKill`). Whether a pentakill also adds one to the double, triple and quadra counters ("nested") or only to the pentakill counter ("separate") is not documented by Riot, and could not be established from the repository: it holds no real Riot match payload (`tests/fixtures/riot-game*.ts` are scripted games whose counters are all zero, `src/server/riot/browser-test.ts` a scripted double), and the stored timelines keep each player's kill times but not Riot's `CHAMPION_SPECIAL_KILL` events. Two public sample payloads (Riot's own match-v5 example and a match-v4 game) are consistent with nested counting (every player with a largest multikill of 3 also has a double counted) but do not prove it. So the stored games decide (`readCountingRule`): nested counters can never show more triples than doubles, more quadras than triples, more pentas than quadras, or a largest multikill of 3 or more without a double (4+ without a triple, 5 without a quadra), while separate counters show that in most games whose one multikill was a triple. With at least 20 player-games with a triple among every participant of the season's stored games, under 1% of them out of that order reads nested (a stray record cannot flip it), 10% or more separate; otherwise (too few, or 1–10%) nested is assumed, the reading that can never count a multikill twice, and the ⓘ says it is assumed. Nested counts become one event each: doubles − triples are the multikills that stopped at a double, triples − quadras at a triple, quadras − pentas at a quadra, and every pentakill once (never below zero). The `data-audit` job reports the same reading on the members' games (`multikills.riotCounting`). The demo's synthetic counters are nested (since Release 15 round 2: each draw, once at its largest, kept as nested counters, no draw added or moved), so the nested path is the one the browser tests and the screenshots exercise; a unit test shows the same games score the same under either rule read correctly. The board shows the events, not the counters: a member's rows and the "Most …" tiles read the same rule as the score (the e2e spec checks each row's shown counts against its `data-riot` counters read nested; so does the Production check, by the rule the ⓘ states).

**2. The comparison: the other players in the same games.** Every stored game record (`match_details.participants`) holds all ten players with their champion, Riot's position and, since Phase 14, the same counters. The comparison is every participant of the members' season games (the very games the season records count: one season's supported Summoner's Rift queues, remakes left out) who is not a team member (no linked member account's PUUID) and not a removed player, with counters: allies and opponents. It is labelled as what it is ("the other players in the same games … allies and opponents, never a team member: 2,383 of their games": player-games, each other player once per game), never as high-elo or global: no such baseline is available (the OD4 high-elo collector is switched off). A seam is left for one: `MultikillBaseline.source` names where a comparison comes from. Only aggregates leave the database: per position and champion, the games and the multikills under both readings, and two counts that tell how Riot counts. No name, PUUID or game.
- **Reliability: at least 1,000 comparison games**, every position together (`BASELINE_MIN_GAMES`). At the rates usually seen (about one double kill every two games, a triple every 15, a quadra every 100) 1,000 games hold about 500 double kills and 65 triple kills, so those rates are known within about 5% and 12% (1/√n), and about 10 quadra kills, within about a third. Pooled by kills (below), a quadra or penta rate a third off moves a 100-game member's score by under 2 points, a triple rate 12% off by under 3 (the tests show both). Under 1,000 the members are compared with the team's own games, every position together, and the board and its ⓘ say so ("not adjusted for role or champion"); the demo has about 2,400 comparison games.
- **Role:** a role's rate for each kind is its events over its games, leaning toward every position's rate by 200 pseudo-games (`ROLE_PRIOR_GAMES`): (role's events + 200 × all positions' rate) ÷ (role's games + 200). A game counts as compared by role once its role has 200 games of its own; a game without a role is compared with every position. The score's sentence says when only some of a member's games were compared by role.
- **Champion:** a champion moves its role's rates by its own multikill tendency, leaning toward the role by 50 pseudo-games (`CHAMPION_PRIOR_GAMES`): factor = (champion's multikills + 50 × role's per-game rate) ÷ ((champion's games + 50) × role's per-game rate). A champion seen in 5 games with twice the role's multikills moves it by 1.09; one with 50 games and 40 multikills in a role of 0.5 a game, by 1.3. The tendency is the same for every kind (stated assumption: a champion with twice the role's multikills is expected to make twice its pentakills too), so a rare kind is never read from a champion's handful of games. All champions do not have equal multikill potential, and the comparison says so per champion, but only as far as its games show.

**3. The score** (Release 15 round 2, cushion from round 3; the first version averaged four per-kind indexes with equal weight). The member's multikill kills against the kills expected, pooled:

    points   = 2·O_double + 3·O_triple + 4·O_quadra + 5·O_penta        expected = 2·X_double + 3·X_triple + 4·X_quadra + 5·X_penta
    score    = 100 × (points + 40) ÷ (expected + 40)
    chance   = 100 × √(4·X_double + 9·X_triple + 16·X_quadra + 25·X_penta) ÷ (expected + 40)

O_k is the member's multikills of kind k, each once at its largest; X_k the number expected (each counted game's rate for its role and champion, added up). `chance` is the score's spread by chance alone at the member's games (one standard deviation at exactly the usual rates; each kind's count varies like a Poisson count, weighted by its kills): the Peek shows it ("±9"), and the ⓘ quotes it at 50 and 300 games from the comparison's own all-position rates (`typicalChance`), and the range within which two scores could swap by luck: about √2 × the 50-game spread (the spread of two scores' difference). Because of the cushion, "130" is about, not exactly, 30% more.
- **Why pooled.** The review of the first version simulated it: with the four kinds' indexes averaged with equal weight, the score's chance spread stayed at about 12 points at 20, 50, 100, 300 and 1,000 games, and a truly 30%-better member averaged only 110–124, because the quadra and penta indexes stay mostly luck at any sample size (one pentakill was 12 points at 20 games, 8 at 500, worth about 85 doubles). Pooled, every kill in a multikill is evidence in proportion to how often it comes, so the common kinds, which hold most of the information, carry most of the weight, and the spread falls with games.
- **Why kills as weights** (`MULTIKILL_WEIGHTS`): a rarer kind counts for more by what it is (a pentakill is five kills in one fight, 2.5 double kills' worth; a triple 1.5), a rule anyone can check, not a constant chosen for it. **The trade-off, plainly:** a pentakill counts as 2.5 doubles, not as the rarity it is, so over many games a score is mostly doubles and triples; pentakills are recognised on their own by the "Most pentas" tile (and in each row's words and the Peek), not by the score.
- **Why a cushion of 40 kill points** (`MULTIKILL_CUSHION`, as if every member had already made twenty double kills at exactly the usual rate; round 3, chosen by simulation against 20, 30 and 60). It is fixed in kill points, so a member whose role makes fewer multikills (a support: about 0.35 kill points a game against a mid's 1.4) leans on it for more games, as their games carry less information. A single pentakill moves a ranked 20-game mid's score by about 7 points, a 500-game one's by under 1. The choice, by cushion (one unseeded run of each, 800 to 2,000 boards per figure):

  | Cushion | Equal skill, four 20-game and four 300-game members: a 20-game member first (no bias: 50%) | 9 members about 10% apart: members with 30 games or fewer on top (their true share) | 8 members about 20% apart: 20-game members on top (true) | Truly 30% better at 20 / 50 / 300 games | Spread at 20 / 300 games |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | 20 (round 2) | 77% | 42% (33%) | 24% (25%) | 115 / 122 / 128 | 16.5 / 7.3 |
  | 30 | 71% | 38% (32%) | 20% (26%) | 114 / 120 / 127 | 13.5 / 7.1 |
  | **40 (chosen)** | **66%** | **34% (35%)** | **20% (25%)** | **111 / 118 / 127** | **11.5 / 6.9** |
  | 60 | 61% | 29% (33%) | 15% (26%) | 109 / 115 / 127 | 8.7 / 6.5 |

  40 removes the bias on boards whose members are about 10% apart (closest to a team's real spread) and cuts the equal-skill excess from 77% to 66%; the rest of that excess stays at any cushion, because a 20-game record is noisier than a 300-game one. Its cost: a truly better member's score converges a little more slowly (118 at 50 games against 122), and on boards 20% apart 20-game members top a little less than their true share (20% against 25%). 60 would hold short records back on both realistic boards.
- **The seeded simulation** (`src/domain/multikill-score.test.ts`, "the score's reliability, simulated (seeded)": the real `scoreMember`, a mid's true rates double 0.5, triple 0.067, quadra 0.01, penta 0.002 a game, Poisson counts, the same numbers on every run):

  | Games | Usual member: mean, spread (SD) | `chance` the page quotes | Truly 30% better: mean |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 20 | 99.9, 11.3 | 11.5 | 111.7 |
  | 50 | 100.1, 11.9 | 11.6 | 117.8 |
  | 100 | 99.6, 10.0 | 10.2 | 124.4 |
  | 300 | 100.1, 7.3 | 7.0 | 126.3 |

  From 50 games on the spread falls with every step; at 20 the cushion holds it near the 50-game spread (a short record leans on it most), which is why the ⓘ says a short record can't top the board on one lucky run rather than that its range is wider. Its boards: 8 members 20% apart, the two 20-game members on top 16.5% of the time (true 24%); four 20-game and four 300-game members of equal skill, a 20-game member first 68%; 9 members 10% apart, those with 30 games or fewer on top 31% (true 32%). The test asserts the spread falls from 50 games on (and at 20 stays within 1.5 points of the 50-game spread, under 14), a truly better member's expected score rises toward 130, `chance` matches the simulated spread within 1.5 points, 20-game members top at most 32% of the 8-member boards and within 10 points of their true share, at most 70% of the equal-skill boards, and on the 9-member boards within 6 points of their true share.

- **Worked examples** (from the tests): 40 games against role rates 0.5, 0.06, 0.01, 0.002 a game, made 26 doubles, 4 triples, 1 quadra, no pentakill: 68 kill points against 49.2 expected (20, 2.4, 0.4, 0.08), score 100 × 108 ÷ 89.2 = **121**, ±12 by chance. A support with 200 games and no pentakill, 40 doubles, 4 triples and a quadra (the support role's usual about 32, 2.7, 0.36, 0.05): **119**, ranked. A mid with 12 games and one pentakill, 6 doubles and a triple: **107**, provisional, listed below. The same 30 double kills in 200 games score 93 as a support and 34 as a mid. A jungler at the jungle's usual rates over 120 games scores about 100.

**4. Places.** 20 or more games with counts (`MULTIKILL_RANKED_GAMES`) for a place; 10–19 provisional (`MULTIKILL_PROVISIONAL_GAMES`), listed after every ranked member; fewer, no score. Equal scores as shown (whole points) share a place; more counted games, then the name, order a tie's rows. The counts beside a score, and the most of each kind, are each multikill once at its largest (the score's own events), as on the profiles since round 3 (below).

**5. One count everywhere** (round 3). How Riot's counters count is read once for every page, from the linked players' own stored games (`server/accounts/multikill-counting.ts`: one aggregate statement over `account_matches`, two counts, cached for 6 hours), and both Team Leaderboards (board, tiles, Peek) and the profiles (Overview's multikill figures, All Champions' and the champion window's per-champion multikills, the season card's line) show each multikill once, at its largest, by it (`multikillsOfGames(games, rule)`). Before, the board said Demo Jungle had 32 doubles and "Full profile", one tap away, Riot's raw 46. Riot's raw counters stay on each board row for the record (`data-riot`). Checked by a DB test (a member's Overview figures equal their board row) and the profile spec (the board's first ranked member's Overview figures equal the row's).

**Cost.** The comparison unpacks the season's stored records once per computation: about 300 ms of database time for 6,000 records of production's shape (54,000 comparison player-games, 800 position-and-champion groups; PostgreSQL 16 on the shared development machine, 23 Nov 2026), its answer about 37 KB. It is cached per team and season for up to 6 hours and recomputed when the team's stored history has changed and the cached one is at least an hour old (a comparison of thousands of games moves little with a few more); a cached read is about 1 ms. The production probe times it (`leaderboards.multikill-baseline`, counts only). The members' page and the public one each read it after the team's data, for the records' season.

## Who can see it, who is on it

- **Open to:** team members with the *view teammate stats* ability. The page checks this on the server (`requirePageAccess("view-teammate-stats")`); the navigation link uses the same check. Personal-use accounts have neither.
- **On the board:** every active team member with a player profile, the same list as the Players page:
  - members still finishing their account setup are listed too (usually under "Not enough recent games", until their games arrive);
  - archived profiles and personal-use profiles are left out;
  - the Owner's admin identity has no player profile, so it never appears.
- **Membership changes:** take effect on the next page load (see *Updates and caching*).

## The public Leaderboards

`/public/leaderboards` shows the same page among the players the public Team page shows, by display name, linking to no profile or game. It exists only where the deployment allows Riot data on the public pages (a Production key whose registration describes them); otherwise the address is not found. Its page is at most 97rem (the public layout's padding inside its 100rem), so from 1440 wide it stands in two columns, its records' column 27.25rem at 1440 and 37.25rem from 1920 (two-line cells there), the milestones right under Recent form (measured with the board switched on, 19 Nov 2026). The old Records address lands on it as on the members' page, and Back after a link returns to it (see *Older addresses*).

## Updates and caching

- **No Riot requests on page open.** The page reads only what the existing account syncs have stored (`account_matches`, `account_mastery`). New games appear after the next account sync; the data status behind the ⓘ beside the member count, on the title's line, says how current the members' games are.
- **Per-game figures are copied once.** When a match's details are stored, each linked player's side, gold, wards, team kills, team damage and extra figures are copied onto their game row (`account_matches`, migration 0033). The leaderboard reads these plain columns instead of re-reading every match's JSON. Any game whose details arrived without the copy (the existing history, once, after the feature first deploys) is copied on the next sync or page load.
- **One read per team, cached.** The games are read in one query and cached per team for up to 15 minutes. The cache is dropped as soon as the set of linked accounts, the number of stored games, the newest game, the newest copied figures or the last sync time changes.
- **Rankings are cheap.** They are computed in memory from the cached games, a few milliseconds for every board at once: the page builds Recent form and the season's records and achievements on every request from the one load (`buildLeaderboardsView`), with no query of either's own. The production probe still times each part on its own (`leaderboards.recent-form`, `leaderboards.records`).
- **One page, one render (19 Nov 2026).** Measured on the demo build, warm, on a shared machine (18 Nov 2026, the mean of five requests each): the one page sends 45.1 KB compressed (548 KB uncompressed) with its first byte after 64 ms, where the two views it replaced sent 27.7 KB in 46 ms (Recent form) and 30.5 KB in 115 ms (Records & achievements). A member who looked at both cost the server two renders (the switch between the views was the second); now one. The seven record cells' games, on the page at every visit now, are not prefetched: their viewport prefetches were 13 requests on a desktop visit (each a function invocation, about 45–105 ms of server CPU together, measured on the demo build), so a record's game loads on the click instead.

## Privacy

- **What the board uses:** Riot match figures and mastery only.
- **What it never reads:** the records kept from the retired Ranked Development (notes, reviews, objectives and plans).
- **Production probe:** the `perf-probe` job records counts only (players, games, games with details, the multikill comparison's games and groups), never names or values.
- **The multikill comparison** reads other players' figures in the members' stored games as aggregates only (per position and champion): no other player's name, PUUID or game is read into the page or shown.

## Data changes

- **Phase 14: no migration.** The extra per-game figures go into the existing `match_details.participants` JSON (an optional `stats` object).
- **Phase 15: migration 0033 (additive).** Nullable columns on `account_matches` (`team_id`, `gold`, `wards_placed`, `team_kills`, `team_damage`, `extra_stats`, `summarised_at`) and a partial index for games still waiting for their copy. No existing value changes; the columns are filled from the stored match details.
- **New syncs:** the Riot match schema parser accepts the extra fields as optional.
- **Demo mode** (`UNIIVERSE_SEED_DEMO`, in-memory database only) seeds **synthetic** games (match ids starting `DEMO_`) so the page has something to show. These are never real player data and are never written to a real database.
