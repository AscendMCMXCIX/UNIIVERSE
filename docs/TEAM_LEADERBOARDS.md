# Team Leaderboards

Friendly competition on the team's own Riot games: **Team → Leaderboards** (`/leaderboards`), between Players and Matches. The rules, and why, are in `docs/LEADERBOARDS_MODEL.md`.

## The page

One page since #72 (19 Nov 2026), with no views or tabs to switch between: Recent form leads (how members are doing lately), the season's records and achievements follow (what they have done, which rewards playing more). Everything is built from the one load of the team's data (see *Updates and caching*); neither part has a query of its own.

- **The header:** the title ("Leaderboards", the page's h1), the member count and the team's data status behind its ⓘ, on one line. The public board shows when the boards were last read instead of the status.
- **Every board's first row is its title** (`BoardTitle`): an icon, the heading (the page's h2s: Recent form, Season records, Multikills, Milestones), an ⓘ where there is more to say, and one short line under it saying what the board counts. No paragraph of explanation stands on the page.
- **Recent form,** the page's lead card (`surface-lead`, the only one on the page): one board. Its title row says "Latest 20 games from the last 60 days · Summoner's Rift, 5 minutes or longer"; its ⓘ ("How Recent form counts") holds what was a footnote under the board: 20 games is a short sample, places within 2 wins are close, the gaps assume the players above don't play in the meantime, each bar counts the window's games, wins first, and which games count.
  - **The window:** each member's latest 20 Summoner's Rift games from the last 60 days, 5 minutes or longer, from every linked account (a match seen from two accounts counts once). Ranked by win rate.
  - **Places:** equal win rates share a place: "3=", titled and read out "Tied for 3rd", with a bracket joining the tied rows in the place column.
  - **Each row says what counts:** the record ("13W 7L") and the same record as a bar of 20 segments (wins first, a count, not the order of the games; a provisional window's missing games outlined), the win rate, the dates the 20 games cover, the main role in those games, and the gap to the place above, short: "1 win behind", or "Next win: 3=" (the place one win would share). The full sentence ("1 win behind Ash", "Win your next game to draw level with Ash") is read out by screen readers and shown on hover (on touch screens only screen readers have it).
  - **Fewer games:** members with 10 to 19 recent games are provisional, listed after every full window ("14 of 20 games"). Members with fewer are the board's last group (rows on phones, chips on wider boards), each with the games they still need and the date of their last game, linked to their profile.
  - **Layout:** the board follows its own width: one line per member from a 58rem board, the name over the dates from 36rem, with the main role's name before the dates from 40rem, two lines on phones (the role as a badge on the picture, the bar as a line at the row's foot). It is at most 72rem wide (`--measure`), at the page's left edge. The whole row opens the member's profile.
  - **Styling:** neutral. No red for a cold streak, and the bar is in the text's own grey; only first place's crown and your own row are marked.
- **Season records,** a plain panel: its title row says "Season 2026 · All supported games · 277 games", its ⓘ ("How these records count") says that records reward playing more (every game is another chance), every record's definition and that a tie goes to whoever set it first (D6), and where there are two seasons to choose from, the season's choice stands at the row's end (`?season=`, the records', the multikills' and the champion games' season; Recent form and all-time mastery take none).
  - **Seven records:** most kills and most assists; damage, CS and vision per minute (from games of 15 minutes or more); best KDA (from games with 10 or more takedowns); the longest win streak.
  - **Each record a cell** of one grid: one column, two from a 44rem panel, the seventh (the streak) across both, so no cell stands empty; a hairline between neighbours. Each cell measures itself (`@container/list` on the cell, its column template inside it), so a record reads the same wherever it stands: two lines beside its picture from a 36rem cell (the record and its qualifier over the holder, champion, role, K/D/A and date; 64px at 100% where each fits its line), three lines under it (the record and its qualifier; the holder, the value at that line's end; the champion, role, K/D/A and date: about 77px), one column under 16rem (a phone at 150% or 200% text: the picture over the record, the holder, the game and the value with its unit, which wrap).
  - **Each cell:** the champion the record was set on (48px; the streak, set over several games, has its mark), the record with its qualifier ("to champions · 15+ min games", "15+ min games", "10+ takedowns", "in game order"; kills and assists need none), its holder as their card shows them (their picture and name; in a narrow cell the holder by name, the cell's picture being the champion's), the champion, role, K/D/A and date, the value with its unit, and the viewer's own best ("Your best: 9 · 4 Oct 2026", or "You hold this record" for its holder; nothing for a viewer with no game that sets it). Your best is a member's line: a fourth line in a narrow cell, under the value from 36rem. The heights above are a viewer's without a profile (the demo's local owner, the verification account).
  - **The whole cell** opens the game in the holder's Match History (`?match=`), with a chevron from a 36rem cell; the streak opens none.
- **Multikills,** "Multikills · Season 2026", rarest first, a row per member: from a 21rem list one line, the member then the three counts under their headings; under it the counts on a line of their own, each with its name. The title row's note says when not every game carries Riot's multikill figures (which games the counts come from, the others left out, not counted as zero). The whole row opens the member's profile.
- **Milestones:** champions with 25, 50 or 100 games this season, and Riot mastery of 100K, 500K or 1M points (all-time), each kind's thresholds behind the ⓘ beside its caption.
  - **A row per member:** their name, then one line of chips (the champion's portrait, its name from a 32rem list, the figure as `format.ts` shortens it: "486K", "1.2M", "29"), the best three, then "+3 more" (the rest are on the profile).
  - **On a phone** the chips show no champion's name (D6): the portrait says which, and screen readers hear the name, the figure in words and the milestone.
  - **The whole row** opens the member's profile; its tooltip names each chip's milestone.
- **Layout,** by the page's own width (`@container/dash`), never the window's; each board keeps its own height (a short board never stretches beside a tall one):
  - **under 59rem, one column** (phones upright and sideways, tablets): the header, the index of the sections below Recent form ("Season records · Multikills · Milestones", `SectionIndex`: each jump brings its section under the top bar and focuses it, an entry the phone's Back steps past), Recent form, the season records (two columns of cells on a tablet), the multikills, the milestones;
  - **from 59rem** (1280 and 1440 wide): Recent form across the page (at most the measure), the season records beside the multikills (21–24rem), the milestones across under both;
  - **from 86rem** (1920, 2560 and 3440 wide): Recent form (58–61rem, its one-line rows) beside the season records (26.5–38.5rem: three-line cells at 1920 × 1080 with the sidebar open, two-line cells with it folded and from 2560), the multikills (22–25rem) under Recent form's start, and the milestones beside them to the page's end.

  At 1920 × 1080 every ranking row and every record is on the first screen, with the achievements starting under them; at 2560 × 1440 the multikills too, and the page is about 1.05 screens; at 3440 × 1440 (the root's 22px cap) about 1.1. With the sidebar open at 1920 the records' column is about 67px taller than Recent form, the price of Recent form keeping its one-line rows beside it. The page is never wider than the window, at 320px and at 200% text included.

**Older addresses.** Records & achievements was a view of its own at `/leaderboards?view=records` until #72: that address opens the same page at its season records (the view leaves the address, the page's own replacing it in the same history entry, so Back leaves the page and Forward returns to it; the section is in view under the bars and focused, as the index's jump does); the season stays. Any other `view` opens the page at its top, and today's `?category=`, `?metric=`, `?queue=`, `?role=` and `?lineup=` open it with nothing they asked for applied. The server never reads `view`: without the page's code the old address simply opens the page.

## Who can see it, who is on it

- **Open to:** team members with the *view teammate stats* ability. The page checks this on the server (`requirePageAccess("view-teammate-stats")`); the navigation link uses the same check. Personal-use accounts have neither.
- **On the board:** every active team member with a player profile, the same list as the Players page:
  - members still finishing their account setup are listed too (usually under "Not enough recent games", until their games arrive);
  - archived profiles and personal-use profiles are left out;
  - the Owner's admin identity has no player profile, so it never appears.
- **Membership changes:** take effect on the next page load (see *Updates and caching*).

## The public Leaderboards

`/public/leaderboards` shows the same page among the players the public Team page shows, by display name, linking to no profile or game. It exists only where the deployment allows Riot data on the public pages (a Production key whose registration describes them); otherwise the address is not found.

## Updates and caching

- **No Riot requests on page open.** The page reads only what the existing account syncs have stored (`account_matches`, `account_mastery`). New games appear after the next account sync; the data status behind the ⓘ under the title says how current the members' games are.
- **Per-game figures are copied once.** When a match's details are stored, each linked player's side, gold, wards, team kills, team damage and extra figures are copied onto their game row (`account_matches`, migration 0033). The leaderboard reads these plain columns instead of re-reading every match's JSON. Any game whose details arrived without the copy (the existing history, once, after the feature first deploys) is copied on the next sync or page load.
- **One read per team, cached.** The games are read in one query and cached per team for up to 15 minutes. The cache is dropped as soon as the set of linked accounts, the number of stored games, the newest game, the newest copied figures or the last sync time changes.
- **Rankings are cheap.** They are computed in memory from the cached games, a few milliseconds for every board at once: the page builds Recent form and the season's records and achievements on every request from the one load (`buildLeaderboardsView`), with no query of either's own. The production probe still times each part on its own (`leaderboards.recent-form`, `leaderboards.records`).
- **One page, one render (#72).** Measured on the demo build, warm, on a shared machine (18 Nov 2026, the mean of five requests each): the one page sends 45.1 KB compressed (548 KB uncompressed) with its first byte after 64 ms, where the two views it replaced sent 27.7 KB in 46 ms (Recent form) and 30.5 KB in 115 ms (Records & achievements). A member who looked at both cost the server two renders (the switch between the views was the second); now one.

## Privacy

- **What the board uses:** Riot match figures and mastery only.
- **What it never reads:** the records kept from the retired Ranked Development (notes, reviews, objectives and plans).
- **Production probe:** the `perf-probe` job records counts only (players, games, games with details), never names or values.

## Data changes

- **Phase 14: no migration.** The extra per-game figures go into the existing `match_details.participants` JSON (an optional `stats` object).
- **Phase 15: migration 0033 (additive).** Nullable columns on `account_matches` (`team_id`, `gold`, `wards_placed`, `team_kills`, `team_damage`, `extra_stats`, `summarised_at`) and a partial index for games still waiting for their copy. No existing value changes; the columns are filled from the stored match details.
- **New syncs:** the Riot match schema parser accepts the extra fields as optional.
- **Demo mode** (`UNIIVERSE_SEED_DEMO`, in-memory database only) seeds **synthetic** games (match ids starting `DEMO_`) so the page has something to show. These are never real player data and are never written to a real database.
