# Team Leaderboards

Friendly competition on the team's own Riot games: **Team → Leaderboards** (`/leaderboards`), between Players and Matches. The rules, and why, are in `docs/LEADERBOARDS_MODEL.md`.

## The page

One page since 19 Nov 2026, one competitive dashboard since 25 Nov 2026 (the owner's requirements of that day): Recent form (how players are doing lately), the multikills, the season records and the milestones, with no views or tabs to switch between, nothing folded and nothing scrolling inside the page. Everything is built from the one load of the team's data (see *Updates and caching*); no part has a query of its own.

- **Who is shown: active players only** (25 Nov 2026). A player appears on any board only while they have BOTH a game the boards count (a supported Summoner's Rift queue, not a remake) that started within the last 30 days, AND a successful data update of one of their accounts within the same 30 days (`riot_accounts.sync_cursor`, written on success only; a failed attempt moves `last_sync_at`, never the cursor; a demo account's seeded time stands for it). A sync that stored no new game never makes a player active: the rule needs gameplay. ## The multikill score

What the multikill board ranks (Release 15, 23 Nov 2026, at the owner's request: "a fairer overall multikill ranking, not most pentakills wins"). The code is `src/domain/multikill-score.ts` (pure functions, worked examples in its tests), the board in `src/domain/leaderboard-records.ts` (`buildMultikillBoard`), the comparison's query in `src/server/leaderboards/multikill-baseline.ts`.

**In plain words.** How often a player makes multikills, per game, against what players on the same roles and champions make in the same stored games. Below 100 is below typical, 100 typical, above 100 above typical; it is not a percentage improvement (the +40 cushion keeps every score nearer 100 than the raw ratio). Every multikill counts once, at its largest, worth its kills (a double kill 2, a pentakill 5), so a rarer kind counts for more. With few games a score stays close to 100, so one lucky game cannot decide the board, and the more games, the less a score owes to chance. The board and the profiles show each multikill once, as the score counts it; the score never credits a multikill nobody made.

**1. Riot's counters, each multikill once.** Riot keeps four counters per player and game (`doubleKills`, `tripleKills`, `quadraKills`, `pentaKills`, with `largestMultiKill`). Whether a pentakill also adds one to the double, triple and quadra counters ("nested") or only to the pentakill counter ("separate") is not documented by Riot, and could not be established from the repository: it holds no real Riot match payload (`tests/fixtures/riot-game*.ts` are scripted games whose counters are all zero, `src/server/riot/browser-test.ts` a scripted double), and the stored timelines keep each player's kill times but not Riot's `CHAMPION_SPECIAL_KILL` events. Two public sample payloads (Riot's own match-v5 example and a match-v4 game) are consistent with nested counting (every player with a largest multikill of 3 also has a double counted) but do not prove it. So the stored games decide (`readCountingRule`): nested counters can never show more triples than doubles, more quadras than triples, more pentas than quadras, or a largest multikill of 3 or more without a double (4+ without a triple, 5 without a quadra), while separate counters show that in most games whose one multikill was a triple. With at least 20 player-games with a triple among every participant of the season's stored games, under 1% of them out of that order reads nested (a stray record cannot flip it), 10% or more separate; otherwise (too few, or 1–10%) nested is assumed, the reading that can never count a multikill twice, and the breakdown says it is assumed. Nested counts become one event each: doubles − triples are the multikills that stopped at a double, triples − quadras at a triple, quadras − pentas at a quadra, and every pentakill once (never below zero). The `data-audit` job reports the same reading on the members' games (`multikills.riotCounting`). The demo's synthetic counters are nested (since Release 15 round 2: each draw, once at its largest, kept as nested counters, no draw added or moved), so the nested path is the one the browser tests and the screenshots exercise; a unit test shows the same games score the same under either rule read correctly. The board shows the events, not the counters: a player's row reads the same rule as the score (the e2e spec checks each row's shown counts against its `data-riot` counters read nested; so does the Production check, by the rule the board states as data, `data-counting-rule`).

**2. The comparison: the other players in the same games.** Every stored game record (`match_details.participants`) holds all ten players with their champion, Riot's position and, since Phase 14, the same counters. The comparison is every participant of the members' season games (the very games the season records count: one season's supported Summoner's Rift queues, remakes left out) who is not a team member (no linked member account's PUUID) and not a removed player, with counters: allies and opponents. It is labelled as what it is ("the other players in the same games … allies and opponents, never a team member: 2,383 of their games": player-games, each other player once per game), never as high-elo or global: no such baseline is available (the OD4 high-elo collector is switched off). A seam is left for one: `MultikillBaseline.source` names where a comparison comes from. Only aggregates leave the database: per position and champion, the games and the multikills under both readings, and two counts that tell how Riot counts. No name, PUUID or game.
- **Reliability: at least 1,000 comparison games**, every position together (`BASELINE_MIN_GAMES`). At the rates usually seen (about one double kill every two games, a triple every 15, a quadra every 100) 1,000 games hold about 500 double kills and 65 triple kills, so those rates are known within about 5% and 12% (1/√n), and about 10 quadra kills, within about a third. Pooled by kills (below), a quadra or penta rate a third off moves a 100-game member's score by under 2 points, a triple rate 12% off by under 3 (the tests show both). Under 1,000 the members are compared with the team's own games, every position together, and the board, its ⓘ and every breakdown say so ("This score is not champion- or role-adjusted."); the demo has about 2,400 comparison games.
- **Role:** a role's rate for each kind is its events over its games, leaning toward every position's rate by 200 pseudo-games (`ROLE_PRIOR_GAMES`): (role's events + 200 × all positions' rate) ÷ (role's games + 200). A game counts as compared by role once its role has 200 games of its own; a game without a role is compared with every position. The breakdown counts a player's games by how each was compared (below).
- **Champion:** a champion moves its role's rates by its own multikill tendency, leaning toward the role by 50 pseudo-games (`CHAMPION_PRIOR_GAMES`): factor = (champion's multikills + 50 × role's per-game rate) ÷ ((champion's games + 50) × role's per-game rate). A champion seen in 5 games with twice the role's multikills moves it by 1.09; one with 50 games and 40 multikills in a role of 0.5 a game, by 1.3. The tendency is the same for every kind (stated assumption: a champion with twice the role's multikills is expected to make twice its pentakills too), so a rare kind is never read from a champion's handful of games. All champions do not have equal multikill potential, and the comparison says so per champion, but only as far as its games show.

**3. The score** (Release 15 round 2, cushion from round 3; the first version averaged four per-kind indexes with equal weight). The member's multikill kills against the kills expected, pooled:

    points   = 2·O_double + 3·O_triple + 4·O_quadra + 5·O_penta        expected = 2·X_double + 3·X_triple + 4·X_quadra + 5·X_penta
    score    = 100 × (points + 40) ÷ (expected + 40)
    chance   = 100 × √(4·X_double + 9·X_triple + 16·X_quadra + 25·X_penta) ÷ (expected + 40)

O_k is the member's multikills of kind k, each once at its largest; X_k the number expected (each counted game's rate for its role and champion, added up). `chance` is the score's spread by chance alone at the player's games (one standard deviation at exactly the usual rates; each kind's count varies like a Poisson count, weighted by its kills): the breakdown quotes it ("Luck still matters: by chance alone a score like this moves about ±9"). `typicalChance` (the spread at 50 and 300 games from the comparison's own all-position rates) stays for the data audit. The page never calls a score a percentage: because of the cushion it is not one.
- **Why pooled.** The review of the first version simulated it: with the four kinds' indexes averaged with equal weight, the score's chance spread stayed at about 12 points at 20, 50, 100, 300 and 1,000 games, and a truly 30%-better member averaged only 110–124, because the quadra and penta indexes stay mostly luck at any sample size (one pentakill was 12 points at 20 games, 8 at 500, worth about 85 doubles). Pooled, every kill in a multikill is evidence in proportion to how often it comes, so the common kinds, which hold most of the information, carry most of the weight, and the spread falls with games.
- **Why kills as weights** (`MULTIKILL_WEIGHTS`): a rarer kind counts for more by what it is (a pentakill is five kills in one fight, 2.5 double kills' worth; a triple 1.5), a rule anyone can check, not a constant chosen for it. **The trade-off, plainly:** a pentakill counts as 2.5 doubles, not as the rarity it is, so over many games a score is mostly doubles and triples; pentakills are recognised on their own in the table's Pentas column (and the breakdown's points), not by the score.
- **Why a cushion of 40 kill points** (`MULTIKILL_CUSHION`, as if every member had already made twenty double kills at exactly the usual rate; round 3, chosen by simulation against 20, 30 and 60). It is fixed in kill points, so a member whose role makes fewer multikills (a support: about 0.35 kill points a game against a mid's 1.4) leans on it for more games, as their games carry less information. A single pentakill moves a ranked 20-game mid's score by about 7 points, a 500-game one's by under 1. The choice, by cushion (one unseeded run of each, 800 to 2,000 boards per figure):

  | Cushion | Equal skill, four 20-game and four 300-game members: a 20-game member first (no bias: 50%) | 9 members about 10% apart: members with 30 games or fewer on top (their true share) | 8 members about 20% apart: 20-game members on top (true) | Truly 30% better at 20 / 50 / 300 games | Spread at 20 / 300 games |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | 20 (round 2) | 77% | 42% (33%) | 24% (25%) | 115 / 122 / 128 | 16.5 / 7.3 |
  | 30 | 71% | 38% (32%) | 20% (26%) | 114 / 120 / 127 | 13.5 / 7.1 |
  | **40 (chosen)** | **66%** | **34% (35%)** | **20% (25%)** | **111 / 118 / 127** | **11.5 / 6.9** |
  | 60 | 61% | 29% (33%) | 15% (26%) | 109 / 115 / 127 | 8.7 / 6.5 |

  40 removes the bias on boards whose members are about 10% apart (closest to a team's real spread) and cuts the equal-skill excess from 77% to 66%; the rest of that excess stays at any cushion, because a 20-game record is noisier than a 300-game one. Its cost: a truly better member's score converges a little more slowly (118 at 50 games against 122), and on boards 20% apart 20-game members top a little less than their true share (20% against 25%). 60 would hold short records back on both realistic boards.
- **The seeded simulation** (`src/domain/multikill-score.test.ts`, "the score's reliability, simulated (seeded)": the real `scoreMember`, a mid's true rates double 0.5, triple 0.067, quadra 0.01, penta 0.002 a game, Poisson counts, the same numbers on every run):

  | Games | Usual member: mean, spread (SD) | `chance` the page quotes | Truly 30% better: mean |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 20 | 99.9, 11.3 | 11.5 | 111.7 |
  | 50 | 100.1, 11.9 | 11.6 | 117.8 |
  | 100 | 99.6, 10.0 | 10.2 | 124.4 |
  | 300 | 100.1, 7.3 | 7.0 | 126.3 |

  From 50 games on the spread falls with every step; at 20 the cushion holds it near the 50-game spread (a short record leans on it most), which is why a short record cannot top the board on one lucky run (the breakdown says players with fewer games are kept closer to 100) rather than its range being wider. Its boards: 8 members 20% apart, the two 20-game members on top 16.5% of the time (true 24%); four 20-game and four 300-game members of equal skill, a 20-game member first 68%; 9 members 10% apart, those with 30 games or fewer on top 31% (true 32%). The test asserts the spread falls from 50 games on (and at 20 stays within 1.5 points of the 50-game spread, under 14), a truly better member's expected score rises toward 130, `chance` matches the simulated spread within 1.5 points, 20-game members top at most 32% of the 8-member boards and within 10 points of their true share, at most 70% of the equal-skill boards, and on the 9-member boards within 6 points of their true share.

- **Worked examples** (from the tests): 40 games against role rates 0.5, 0.06, 0.01, 0.002 a game, made 26 doubles, 4 triples, 1 quadra, no pentakill: 68 kill points against 49.2 expected (20, 2.4, 0.4, 0.08), score 100 × 108 ÷ 89.2 = **121**, ±12 by chance. A support with 200 games and no pentakill, 40 doubles, 4 triples and a quadra (the support role's usual about 32, 2.7, 0.36, 0.05): **119**, ranked. A mid with 12 games and one pentakill, 6 doubles and a triple: **107**, provisional (10–19 games): not listed until 20 games with counts. The same 30 double kills in 200 games score 93 as a support and 34 as a mid. A jungler at the jungle's usual rates over 120 games scores about 100.

**4. Places.** 20 or more games with counts (`MULTIKILL_RANKED_GAMES`) for a place; 10–19 provisional (`MULTIKILL_PROVISIONAL_GAMES`) and fewer, no place: since 25 Nov 2026 the board lists ranked players only (and only active ones), placed among themselves, so no place is skipped (the thresholds stay in the model). Equal scores as shown (whole points) share a place; more counted games, then the name, order a tie's rows. The counts beside a score are each multikill once at its largest (the score's own events), as on the profiles since round 3 (below).

**5. One count everywhere** (round 3). How Riot's counters count is read once for every page, from the linked players' own stored games (`server/accounts/multikill-counting.ts`: one aggregate statement over `account_matches`, two counts, cached for 6 hours), and both Team Leaderboards (the table, the breakdown, the Peek) and the profiles (Overview's multikill figures, All Champions' and the champion window's per-champion multikills, the season card's line) show each multikill once, at its largest, by it (`multikillsOfGames(games, rule)`). Riot's raw counters stay on each board row for the record (`data-riot`). Checked by a DB test (a member's Overview figures equal their board row) and the profile spec (the board's first ranked member's Overview figures equal the row's).

**6. The Score breakdown** (25 Nov 2026). Each Score is a button (the shared breakdown popover, `src/components/ui/breakdown-popover.tsx`: beside the Score on wider screens, up to the window's height so it opens whole; a sheet on phones and short windows) showing how that score was worked out, every number from the score itself (`scoreMember`'s parts, formatted on the server in `recent-view.ts`; `multikill-breakdown.tsx` works nothing out):
- the score, the place among the ranked players ("3rd of 5", "Tied for 2nd of 5") and the games behind it ("40 games with multikill counts"; when some season games carry no counts, "4 of 44 season games have no multikill counts and are left out, never counted as zero");
- the most-played role over those games with every position's share, and the most-played champions (up to four, each with the position played on it and its games);
- the points per kind ("Double kills 29 × 2 58" …), the total earned, the points typical for these champions and roles (`expectedPoints`, one decimal, or as many as it takes for the final line to give the score shown: `expectedPointsText`, two or three decimals in about one score in a hundred), the small-sample adjustment "+40 to both" with "Players with fewer games are kept closer to 100 so a few unusually good or bad games have less influence.", and the final line **100 × (80 + 40) ÷ (67.1 + 40) = 112**, whose terms reproduce the score shown (a unit test over 2,000 random members; the Production check recomputes it on production);
- where typical comes from and its size ("Typical results come from 2,383 games of other players in the team's stored season matches, excluding team members — not every League player."), and how this player's games were compared: **by champion and role** (the role's players with 200+ games of their own and the champion with 50+ games on that role, `CHAMPION_PRIOR_GAMES`: its tendency carries at least half the weight), **by role** (the champion seen in fewer than 50 games, which barely moves the rates), **all positions together** (no position recorded, or a role with fewer than 200 games), or **the team's own games** (the fallback: then "This score is not champion- or role-adjusted." in plain words) (`comparisonBasis`, with counts per level);
- the limits: the counting rule, luck ("Luck still matters: by chance alone a score like this moves about ±9"), and that all supported Summoner's Rift queues are compared together, per game;
- why placements differ: "Player A earns 80 points where 60 are typical: 100 × (80 + 40) ÷ (60 + 40) = 120. Player B earns 160 where 150 are typical: 100 × (160 + 40) ÷ (150 + 40) = 105. A places higher with fewer points: what counts is how far each is above typical for their champions and roles." On the team-wide fallback it ends "… above the team's typical results.": no line of a breakdown claims a champion or role adjustment the score has not made (the review of 25 Nov 2026 found this one did; `multikill-breakdown.test.ts`, and a Production check fact).
- **The keyboard** (the shared breakdown popover): Enter or Space on the Score opens it with the focus inside; Escape closes it back on the Score; drawn at the page's end, it still keeps the Score's place in the page's order: Tab past its ✕ closes it and goes on to the next row, Shift+Tab out of it closes it back on the Score (the review of 25 Nov 2026: Tab used to leave the page with the breakdown still open, and Shift+Tab landed on the page's last control).

The player Peek a row opens leads with the same breakdown in short (the score, its place and games, the most-played role, the points and the final line: `MultikillPeekBreakdown`), the same parts and words.

**Cost.** The comparison unpacks the season's stored records once per computation: about 300 ms of database time for 6,000 records of production's shape (54,000 comparison player-games, 800 position-and-champion groups; PostgreSQL 16 on the shared development machine, 23 Nov 2026), its answer about 37 KB. It is cached per team and season for up to 6 hours and recomputed when the team's stored history has changed and the cached one is at least an hour old (a comparison of thousands of games moves little with a few more); a cached read is about 1 ms. The production probe times it (`leaderboards.multikill-baseline`, counts only). The members' page and the public one each read it after the team's data, for the records' season.

## Fairness review (25 Nov 2026)

The owner asked for the multikill score to be checked for fairness on every axis below, and for the maths to change only where a weakness is demonstrated, with a test. This review found none that met that bar, so **the formula is unchanged** (`multikill-score.test.ts`'s math tests are untouched); what it found is disclosed in the breakdown and here. Luck is never claimed to be eliminated.

- **Champion choice.** Each game is compared with what the other players in the same stored games made on the same champion and role (the champion's tendency, leaning toward the role by 50 pseudo-games). A rare champion barely moves the rates (shrunk, not gated), so the breakdown counts a game "by champion and role" only from 50 baseline games of that champion on that role (where its own tendency carries at least half the weight) and "by role" below that, with the counts per player. Not a weakness: disclosed per player.
- **Actual role.** Every game uses the position Riot recorded for it (`teamPosition`, else `individualPosition`), never the player's chosen role. A game with no position is compared with every position together (no champion adjustment then: `expectedRates` returns the all-positions rate before the champion step); the breakdown shows those games as "All positions together", and the role icon counts them as "Unknown". Disclosed.
- **Games played.** The +40 cushion keeps short records near 100 (the seeded simulation: a 20-game player tops a board of equal players 66% of the time against 50% unbiased, the rest of the excess being a short record's noise, see *Why a cushion of 40*); a place needs 20 games with counts. Unchanged; the breakdown's plain sentence says it.
- **Game modes and queue mix.** The comparison pools the seven supported Summoner's Rift queues (Ranked Solo/Duo and Flex, Draft, Blind, Swiftplay, Quickplay, Clash) together, per game, not per minute. A player who mostly plays faster, higher-kill modes is compared with rates averaged over the team's own queue mix. Not measured yet, so not changed: a split of the comparison's rates by queue group in the data audit would be the evidence. Disclosed in every breakdown ("All supported Summoner's Rift queues are compared together, per game."). Game length is not controlled either (the same-games design partly does: allies and opponents share each game's length).
- **Small samples.** As *Games played*; the chance spread is quoted per player ("Luck still matters … about ±9").
- **Missing multikill data.** A game whose record has no Riot counters (stored before Phase 14, or "no longer provided") is left out of the score and of the games column, never counted as zero; the breakdown says how many of a player's season games are left out, the board's foot how many of the team's (`multikillCountsOf`, tested).
- **Double counting.** Each multikill counts once, at its largest: Riot's counters are read as nested or separate from the stored games themselves (`readCountingRule`), nested assumed (the reading that can never count one twice) until they can tell; "the same games give the same score under either rule" is a unit test.
- **Duplicate matches and linked accounts.** A match stored on two of a player's linked accounts is one game (`onePerMatch`, keyed by player and match id): `tests/db/leaderboards-active.test.ts` stores five matches on both of a player's accounts and checks the multikill counts, the games and Recent form count each once; `tests/db/leaderboards.test.ts` checks the games. Two different members in one match each keep their own game, as they should. The comparison reads each match once (`match_details.match_id` is its key) and never a team member.
- **Comparison sample sizes.** At least 1,000 other players' games before a champion-and-role comparison (`BASELINE_MIN_GAMES`; the rates known within about 5–12%), else the team-wide fallback, said plainly ("not champion- or role-adjusted"). The breakdown gives the comparison's size ("2,383 games of other players …").
- **Fallbacks.** The team-wide fallback includes the player's own games (self-influence) and has no role or champion adjustment: disclosed, never presented as role-adjusted. A small weakness, also noted: the comparison leaves out current members only, so an archived or personal-use player's games count as "other players"; the effect is a handful of games among thousands.
- **Shared placements.** Places use the score as shown (whole points); equal shown scores share a place (competition ranking: 1, 2, 2, 4); more counted games then the name only order a tie's rows. Unchanged.
- **Inactive players.** Hidden by the 30-day rule on every board, the places counted again among the players shown (no gaps), while the comparison and its fallback still read every member's games, so hiding a player changes nobody else's typical points (a unit test: `recent-view.test.ts`, "an inactive leader leaves no gap"). A returning player is back with their next synced game.

## Who can see it, who is on it

- **Open to:** team members with the *view teammate stats* ability. The page checks this on the server (`requirePageAccess("view-teammate-stats")`); the navigation link uses the same check. Personal-use accounts have neither.
- **On the board:** the team's members with a player profile (the same list as the Players page) who are active: a counted game and a successful data update in the last 30 days (see *Who is shown*):
  - members still finishing their account setup, or away for 30 days, are not shown until they have played and their accounts have been read (nothing of theirs is deleted);
  - archived profiles and personal-use profiles are left out;
  - the Owner's admin identity has no player profile, so it never appears.
- **Membership changes:** take effect on the next page load (see *Updates and caching*).

## Updates and caching

- **No Riot requests on page open.** The page reads only what the existing account syncs have stored (`account_matches`, `account_mastery`). New games appear after the next account sync; the data status behind the ⓘ beside the member count, on the title's line, says how current the members' games are.
- **Per-game figures are copied once.** When a match's details are stored, each linked player's side, gold, wards, team kills, team damage and extra figures are copied onto their game row (`account_matches`, migration 0033). The leaderboard reads these plain columns instead of re-reading every match's JSON. Any game whose details arrived without the copy (the existing history, once, after the feature first deploys) is copied on the next sync or page load.
- **One read per team, cached.** The games are read in one query and cached per team for up to 15 minutes. The cache is dropped as soon as the set of linked accounts, the number of stored games, the newest game, the newest copied figures or the last sync time changes.
- **Rankings are cheap.** They are computed in memory from the cached games, a few milliseconds for every board at once: the page builds Recent form and the season's records and achievements on every request from the one load (`buildLeaderboardsView`), with no query of either's own. The production probe still times each part on its own (`leaderboards.recent-form`, `leaderboards.records`).
- **One page, one render (19 Nov 2026).** Measured on the demo build, warm, on a shared machine (18 Nov 2026, the mean of five requests each): the one page sends 45.1 KB compressed (548 KB uncompressed) with its first byte after 64 ms, where the two views it replaced sent 27.7 KB in 46 ms (Recent form) and 30.5 KB in 115 ms (Records & achievements). A member who looked at both cost the server two renders (the switch between the views was the second); now one. The seven record cells' games, on the page at every visit now, are not prefetched: their viewport prefetches were 13 requests on a desktop visit (each a function invocation, about 45–105 ms of server CPU together, measured on the demo build), so a record's game loads on the click instead.

## Privacy

- **What the board uses:** Riot match figures and mastery only.
- **What it never reads:** the records kept from the retired Ranked Development (notes, reviews, objectives and plans).
- **Production probe:** the `perf-probe` job records counts only (players, games, games with details, the multikill comparison's games and groups), never names or values.
- **The multikill comparison** reads other players' figures in the members' stored games as aggregates only (per position and champion): no other player's name, PUUID or game is read into the page or shown.

## Data changes

- **Phase 14: no migration.** The extra per-game figures go into the existing `match_details.participants` JSON (an optional `stats` object).
- **Phase 15: migration 0033 (additive).** Nullable columns on `account_matches` (`team_id`, `gold`, `wards_placed`, `team_kills`, `team_damage`, `extra_stats`, `summarised_at`) and a partial index for games still waiting for their copy. No existing value changes; the columns are filled from the stored match details.
- **New syncs:** the Riot match schema parser accepts the extra fields as optional.
- **Demo mode** (`UNIIVERSE_SEED_DEMO`, in-memory database only) seeds **synthetic** games (match ids starting `DEMO_`) so the page has something to show. These are never real player data and are never written to a real database.
