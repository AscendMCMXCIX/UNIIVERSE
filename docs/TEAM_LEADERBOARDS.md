# Team Leaderboards

Friendly competition on the team's own Riot games: **Team → Leaderboards** (`/leaderboards`), between Players and Matches. The rules, and why, are in `docs/LEADERBOARDS_MODEL.md`.

## The page

One page since 19 Nov 2026, with no views or tabs to switch between: Recent form leads (how members are doing lately), the season's records and achievements follow (what they have done, which rewards playing more). Everything is built from the one load of the team's data (see *Updates and caching*); neither part has a query of its own.

- **The header:** the title ("Leaderboards", the page's h1), the member count and the team's data status behind its ⓘ, on one line. The public board shows when the boards were last read instead of the status.
- **Every board's first row is its title** (`BoardTitle`): an icon, the heading (the page's h2s: Recent form, Season records, Multikills, Milestones), an ⓘ where there is more to say, and one short line under it saying what the board counts. No paragraph of explanation stands on the page.
- **Recent form,** the page's lead card (`surface-lead`, the only one on the page): one board. Its title row says "Latest 20 games from the last 60 days · Summoner's Rift, 5 minutes or longer"; its ⓘ ("How Recent form counts") holds what was a footnote under the board: 20 games is a short sample, places within 2 wins are close, the gaps assume the players above don't play in the meantime, each bar counts the window's games, wins first, and which games count.
  - **The window:** each member's latest 20 Summoner's Rift games from the last 60 days, 5 minutes or longer, from every linked account (a match seen from two accounts counts once). Ranked by win rate.
  - **Places:** equal win rates share a place: "3=", titled and read out "Tied for 3rd", with a bracket joining the tied rows in the place column.
  - **Each row says what counts:** the record ("13W 7L") and the same record as a bar of 20 segments (wins first, a count, not the order of the games; a provisional window's missing games outlined), the win rate, the dates the 20 games cover, the main role in those games, and the gap to the place above, short: "1 win behind", or "A win ties 3rd" (the place one win would share; "Next win: 3=" until 20 Nov 2026, a token to decode, UD.L5). The full sentence ("1 win behind Ash", "Win your next game to draw level with Ash") is read out by screen readers and shown on hover (on touch screens only screen readers have it).
  - **Fewer games:** members with 10 to 19 recent games are provisional, listed after every full window ("14 of 20 games"). Members with fewer are the board's last group (rows on phones, chips on wider boards), each with the games they still need and the date of their last game, linked to their profile.
  - **Layout:** the board follows its own width: one line per member from a 58rem board, the name over the dates from 36rem, with the main role's name before the dates from 40rem, two lines on phones (the role as a badge on the picture, the bar as a line at the row's foot). It is at most 72rem wide (`--measure`), at the page's left edge. The whole row opens the member's player Peek (`?player=`, PLAN S8a: S4a's Peek with the Team lineup's player content, a sheet on touch phones; Back closes it and the board does not move); the member's name is a link to their profile, one press away. The same on the multikills' and milestones' rows.
  - **At a larger text size** (18 Nov 2026) the board is narrower in rem (on a 320px phone 17.9rem at 100%, 13.9 at 125%, 11.25 at 150%, 9.4 at 175% and 7.9 at 200%), and beside the place, picture and win rate a name kept a letter a line; the gap ("1 win behind") ran 32px past the board. Three tiers, each under any board at the usual size: under 17.5rem the record and the dates take a line each (the "·" between them hidden; "10W 10L ·" had ended lines); under 16.5rem the win rate takes its own line under the name, which gets the text column's whole width; under 13.5rem the place and the picture take the first line, and the name, the win rate and the second line each the row's whole width under them (the role badge stays on the picture and the bar at the row's foot; the tie bracket steps aside, since the names run under it, and "4=" still says the tie). On a board under 36rem the line of a name and "You" wraps, so "You" drops under a name whose longest word would not fit beside it (MemberCell's `youWraps`; "Suppor|t" broke at 125% on a 375px phone, "Christopher" even at 100% on a 320px one) and stays beside it elsewhere. The inline "·" of the "Not enough recent games" rows and of the groups' captions is held to its words by no-break spaces, so it never ends or starts a line.
  - **Styling:** neutral. No red for a cold streak, and the bar is in the text's own grey; only first place's crown and your own row are marked.
- **Season records,** a plain panel: its title row says "Season 2026 · All supported games · 277 games", its ⓘ ("How these records count") says that records reward playing more (every game is another chance), every record's definition and that a tie goes to whoever set it first (D6), and where there are two seasons to choose from, the season's choice stands at the row's end (`?season=`, the records', the multikills' and the champion games' season; Recent form and all-time mastery take none).
  - **Seven records:** most kills and most assists; damage, CS and vision per minute (from games of 15 minutes or more); best KDA (from games with 10 or more takedowns); the longest win streak.
  - **Each record a cell** of one grid: one column, two from a 40rem panel under Recent form (a tablet either way round, a phone held sideways, 1440 wide, 1280 with the sidebar folded; at 1280 with it open the panel is 36rem, where two columns would wrap the holders' names), never from 86rem, where the panel heads the column beside Recent form (26.25–38.5rem) with the multikills under it, the seventh (the streak) across both, so no cell stands empty; a hairline between neighbours. Each cell measures itself (`@container/list` on the cell, its column template inside it), so a record reads the same wherever it stands: two lines beside its picture from a 36rem cell (the record and its qualifier over the holder, champion, role, K/D/A and date; 64px at 100% where each fits its line), three lines under it (the record and its qualifier; the holder, the value at that line's end; the champion, role, K/D/A and date: about 77px), one column under 16rem (a phone at 150% or 200% text: the picture over the record, the holder, the game and the value with its unit, which wrap).
  - **Each cell:** the champion the record was set on (48px; the streak, set over several games, has its mark), the record with its qualifier ("to champions · 15+ min games", "15+ min games", "10+ takedowns", "in game order"; kills and assists need none), its holder as their card shows them (their picture and name; in a narrow cell the holder by name, the cell's picture being the champion's), the champion, role, K/D/A and date, the value with its unit, and the viewer's own best ("Your best: 9 · 4 Oct 2026", or "You hold this record" for its holder; nothing for a viewer with no game that sets it). Your best is a member's line: a fourth line in a narrow cell, under the value from 36rem. The heights above are a viewer's without a profile (the demo's local owner, the verification account); a member with a best on every record sees each narrow cell about 20px taller (the panel 806px against 667px at 1920 × 1080 with the sidebar open), which from 86rem lengthens the records' own column only (see *Layout*).
  - **The whole cell** opens the game in the holder's Match History (`?match=`), with a chevron from a 36rem cell; the streak opens none. The link is not prefetched: the cells are on every visit, and a prefetch of each game in view was a server render of its own (13 requests on a desktop visit, each a function invocation); the game loads on the click.
- **Multikills,** "Multikills · Season 2026", rarest first, a row per member: from a 21rem list one line, the member then the three counts under their headings; under it the counts on a line of their own, each with its name. When not every game carries Riot's multikill figures, the title row's line says it in a few words ("From 255 of 277 games") and its ⓘ the sentence (the others left out, not counted as zero; it ran to three lines on a phone, UM.L1).
- **Milestones:** champions with 25, 50 or 100 games this season, and Riot mastery of 100K, 500K or 1M points (all-time), each kind's thresholds behind the ⓘ beside its caption.
  - **A row per member:** their name, then their top milestone as a chip (the champion's portrait, its name from a 32rem list, the figure as `format.ts` shortens it: "486K", "1.2M", "29") and "+N" for the rest (on the profile), on the name's line (under it in a list under 18rem, a phone at large text). Three chips took a second line on a phone (UM.L1).
  - **On a phone** the chips show no champion's name (D6): the portrait says which, and screen readers hear the name, the figure in words and the milestone.
  - **The whole row** opens the member's player Peek; its tooltip names each of their best three milestones (the row shows the best one and "+N").
- **Layout,** by the page's own width (`@container/dash`), never the window's; each board keeps its own height (a short board never stretches beside a tall one):
  - **under 59rem, one column** (phones upright and sideways, tablets): the header, the index of the sections below Recent form ("Season records · Multikills · Milestones", `SectionIndex`: each jump brings its section under the top bar and focuses it, an entry the phone's Back steps past; on a page under 18rem, a phone from 125% text, its links wrap without the dots, which would start lines, and with room between the lines for each 44px target), Recent form, the season records (two columns of cells on a tablet), the multikills, the milestones;
  - **from 59rem** (1280 and 1440 wide): Recent form across the page (at most the measure), the season records beside the multikills (21–24rem), the milestones under the multikills in their column (since 20 Nov 2026, UD.L2: across under both, they left about 155px empty under the multikills); the section index sits at the header's end (UD.L1: the page is 1.8–2.3 screens there);
  - **from 86rem** (1920, 2560 and 3440 wide; 1512 with the sidebar folded): two columns, each board under the one before it in its own, whatever the other column holds: Recent form (58.25–60rem, its one-line rows) with the milestones under it; beside them the season records (26.25–38.5rem: three-line cells at 1920 × 1080 with the sidebar open, two-line cells with it folded and from 2560) with the multikills under them. Recent form's column is at least 58.25rem: its board's tiers measure inside its 1px border, so its one-line rows need the border and a little more (a 58rem column gave a 57.875rem board, in its medium tier, in a 1512–1515px window with the sidebar folded). The records' column takes the page's growth first, to 38.5rem; its 26.25rem minimum puts the tier's least page at 86rem exactly. Since 19 Nov 2026; before, the multikills and the milestones stood in one row under the taller of Recent form and the records, so a taller records panel left a band empty under Recent form (220px for a member at 1920 open, 210–257px on the public board), and the milestones spanned 62.5–73.5rem beside multikills held to 22–23rem.

  At 1920 × 1080 every ranking row and every record is on the first screen, and the achievements start on it: the milestones are the page's gap (27px) under Recent form, about 325px of them on the first screen with the sidebar open or folded (190px in a maximised 1920 × 947 window). **A record's game over the board (UD.L3, PLAN S6c, 20 Nov 2026).** A record that was set in one game opens that game over the board, at `/leaderboards?match=…` (the season kept): a docked Peek from md, the window on phones and short windows (S4a's Peek), with the result, queue, length, role and date, "Open in Match History" (the game in the holder's Match History), and the post-game view (loadout, figures, lane, notes, scoreboard, folds). ‹ › step through the records' games; the first open pushes one history entry and ‹ › replace it, so ✕, Escape or Back close it on `/leaderboards` (J13: two taps). **Older addresses.** Records & achievements was a view of its own at `/leaderboards?view=records` until 19 Nov 2026: that address opens the same page at its season records, or at the multikills or milestones its old index named (`?view=records#multikills`) (the view leaves the address, the page's own replacing it in the same history entry, so Back leaves the page and Forward returns to it; the section is in view under the bars and focused, as the index's jump does); the season stays. The replace waits for the router to be listening (a task after the page's first effects): run before, on the public board, which has no loading state of its own, it wiped Next's state from the entry, and after a link in the app Back showed the other page under Leaderboards' address. Any other `view` opens the page at its top, and today's `?category=`, `?metric=`, `?queue=`, `?role=` and `?lineup=` open it with nothing they asked for applied. The server never reads `view`: without the page's code the old address simply opens the page.

## Who can see it, who is on it

- **Open to:** team members with the *view teammate stats* ability. The page checks this on the server (`requirePageAccess("view-teammate-stats")`); the navigation link uses the same check. Personal-use accounts have neither.
- **On the board:** every active team member with a player profile, the same list as the Players page:
  - members still finishing their account setup are listed too (usually under "Not enough recent games", until their games arrive);
  - archived profiles and personal-use profiles are left out;
  - the Owner's admin identity has no player profile, so it never appears.
- **Membership changes:** take effect on the next page load (see *Updates and caching*).

## The public Leaderboards

`/public/leaderboards` shows the same page among the players the public Team page shows, by display name, linking to no profile or game. It exists only where the deployment allows Riot data on the public pages (a Production key whose registration describes them); otherwise the address is not found. Its page is at most 97rem (the public layout's padding inside its 100rem), so from 1440 wide it stands in two columns, its records' column 27.25rem at 1440 and 37.25rem from 1920 (two-line cells there), the milestones right under Recent form (measured with the board switched on, 19 Nov 2026). The old Records address lands on it as on the members' page, and Back after a link returns to it (see *Older addresses*).

## Updates and caching

- **No Riot requests on page open.** The page reads only what the existing account syncs have stored (`account_matches`, `account_mastery`). New games appear after the next account sync; the data status behind the ⓘ beside the member count, on the title's line, says how current the members' games are.
- **Per-game figures are copied once.** When a match's details are stored, each linked player's side, gold, wards, team kills, team damage and extra figures are copied onto their game row (`account_matches`, migration 0033). The leaderboard reads these plain columns instead of re-reading every match's JSON. Any game whose details arrived without the copy (the existing history, once, after the feature first deploys) is copied on the next sync or page load.
- **One read per team, cached.** The games are read in one query and cached per team for up to 15 minutes. The cache is dropped as soon as the set of linked accounts, the number of stored games, the newest game, the newest copied figures or the last sync time changes.
- **Rankings are cheap.** They are computed in memory from the cached games, a few milliseconds for every board at once: the page builds Recent form and the season's records and achievements on every request from the one load (`buildLeaderboardsView`), with no query of either's own. The production probe still times each part on its own (`leaderboards.recent-form`, `leaderboards.records`).
- **One page, one render (19 Nov 2026).** Measured on the demo build, warm, on a shared machine (18 Nov 2026, the mean of five requests each): the one page sends 45.1 KB compressed (548 KB uncompressed) with its first byte after 64 ms, where the two views it replaced sent 27.7 KB in 46 ms (Recent form) and 30.5 KB in 115 ms (Records & achievements). A member who looked at both cost the server two renders (the switch between the views was the second); now one. The seven record cells' games, on the page at every visit now, are not prefetched: their viewport prefetches were 13 requests on a desktop visit (each a function invocation, about 45–105 ms of server CPU together, measured on the demo build), so a record's game loads on the click instead.

## Privacy

- **What the board uses:** Riot match figures and mastery only.
- **What it never reads:** the records kept from the retired Ranked Development (notes, reviews, objectives and plans).
- **Production probe:** the `perf-probe` job records counts only (players, games, games with details), never names or values.

## Data changes

- **Phase 14: no migration.** The extra per-game figures go into the existing `match_details.participants` JSON (an optional `stats` object).
- **Phase 15: migration 0033 (additive).** Nullable columns on `account_matches` (`team_id`, `gold`, `wards_placed`, `team_kills`, `team_damage`, `extra_stats`, `summarised_at`) and a partial index for games still waiting for their copy. No existing value changes; the columns are filled from the stored match details.
- **New syncs:** the Riot match schema parser accepts the extra fields as optional.
- **Demo mode** (`UNIIVERSE_SEED_DEMO`, in-memory database only) seeds **synthetic** games (match ids starting `DEMO_`) so the page has something to show. These are never real player data and are never written to a real database.
